**penciled for the browser**, the penciled.org highlighter extension, is a free, open-source (MIT) extension for Chrome, Edge and Firefox that saves highlights and notes on any web page, locally, with no account. This page compares it with other web highlighter extensions: Weava, Glasp, Liner, Diigo, Memex, Hypothesis and Web Highlights. They make different trade-offs, and for many readers one of them is the better choice. To compare JavaScript libraries for your own site instead, see [Compare with other libraries](/compare/).

## When is another tool the better choice?

penciled for the browser does one thing: private highlights and notes on web pages, in one browser. Pick something else when you need:

- **PDFs.** penciled doesn't highlight PDFs. Hypothesis, Glasp and Web Highlights work with PDFs.
- **Cross-device sync.** penciled's highlights stay in the browser you made them in; you can move them with a backup file, but they don't sync. Weava syncs on its free plan, and Web Highlights with a paid plan.
- **Research projects and collaboration.** If you organize sources in shared folders, a research tool fits better: Weava's plans list folders, sub-folders and collaboration.
- **Social reading.** To share highlights, follow other readers or discuss a text in public or in groups, use Glasp or Hypothesis.
- **AI answers and summaries.** penciled has no AI features; Liner and Glasp do.

penciled is not a read-later app or a Pocket replacement: it doesn't save copies of pages, only your highlights and notes on them.

## Where does the penciled.org extension win?

- **Nothing leaves your browser.** No account, no servers, no analytics, and the extension makes no network requests. See the [privacy statement](/extension/privacy/).
- **Free and open source.** MIT licensed, with no paid plan and no limit on highlights. Anyone can [read the source](https://github.com/marcellosso/penciled/tree/main/apps/extension).
- **Pages stay as they are.** Highlights are painted by the browser with the CSS Custom Highlight API instead of wrapping text in elements, so the page's own markup and scripts aren't affected.
- **It doesn't guess.** When a page changed so much that a highlight can't be placed with confidence, it's listed as not found instead of being put on the wrong words.
- **Markdown export.** The notes page exports a `.md` file you can open in Obsidian, Logseq or any notes app. It's a file, not an integration or sync.
